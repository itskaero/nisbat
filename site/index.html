Three screens, one home

The TV, the phones and the browser — all reading the same family lists.

A TV app made for the Chromecast

A light app built for the Chromecast's older browser: it opens instantly, never reloads between screens, and drifts into a calm ambient screen — clock, next prayer, weather, smog, the ayah or sher of the day, and family photos.

An Android app with its own design

Not a website in a wrapper: tabs, bottom sheets, swipe to remove, haptics on the tasbeeh, prayer notifications, and it works offline with the last synced data.

Your phone is the remote

No QR codes, no pairing codes. The first time the app opens, the TV asks “Allow Ali's phone?” — press OK once. After that the phone finds the TV by itself: D-pad or swipe, play/pause, type on the TV, and “Show on TV” for whatever you're looking at.

Run Nisbat at home

On a NAS (ZimaOS, Synology, Unraid…), a Raspberry Pi or a spare laptop, with one docker compose up. The family's lists live there, not in the cloud.

It appears on the TV

Nisbat puts itself on the Chromecast whenever the TV is on its input — and never pulls you away from cable or dish.

Phones join with one OK

Install the app; it finds Nisbat on your Wi-Fi. The TV asks “Allow this phone?” — press OK, and that phone is in for good.

FAQ

Everything you might be wondering

Does my family's data go to the internet?

No. Lists, Quran progress, azkar counts and photos are stored on your own home server. Nisbat only fetches public information — prayer times, weather, the Quran text, news headlines, prices — and never sends your family's data out.

Does it work away from home?

The app opens with the last synced data and keeps your changes; they sync when you're back on the home Wi-Fi. Prayer notifications are scheduled a week ahead, so they still arrive.

Which Chromecast do I need?

Any. Nisbat's TV app is built for the older Chromecast's browser (Chrome 90) and needs no remote — your phone is the remote. Google TV and smart-TV browsers work too.

Will it take over the TV while we watch cable?

No. Nisbat only appears when the TV is already on the Chromecast's input and nothing else is casting. It can also stay off during night hours.

Who can use it?

Only phones someone at home has allowed (once, on the TV or the website), or that know the household PIN. Any phone can be removed again from Settings → Phones.

Why isn't it on the Play Store?

Nisbat is a private family tool that talks to a server in your home, so it's shared as a signed APK. Every release lists its checksum.

What does it cost?

Nothing. There are no accounts, ads or subscriptions.
